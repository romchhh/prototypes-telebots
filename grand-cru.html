about us our projects the process

For fifteen years, Outlander has been the quiet reference point for those who understand what a Land Rover Defender can become — not a replica, not a trend, but a considered object built to last.

We work from a workshop in Scotland, restoring and reimagining classic Defenders with the same materials and sensibility you might find in a tweed mill or a cabinet maker's bench: walnut, teak, tartan, leather, steel.

Every commission begins with the vehicle itself — its history, its scars, its potential. Outlander does not publish a catalogue. We publish projects. Each one picks up where the last left off.

Our projects

Every Outlander vehicle is a one-off commission — conceived, built and finished by hand in Scotland. Each with its own brief, its own character, and its own story on the road.

Commission · 2024

The Highland Defender

A 110 built for long Scottish winters and longer Scottish summers. Matte olive paint, black steel wheels, a walnut and tweed interior woven to order. The brief was simple: a vehicle that belonged on a Highland track at dawn and outside a hotel in Edinburgh by evening.

Six months from first conversation to keys in hand. Chassis stripped, galvanised, rebuilt. TD5 engine refreshed. Every panel aligned by eye, not machine.

Commission · 2023

The Coastal Build

Commissioned for a client who spends half the year on the Atlantic coast. Sand-resistant finishes, a teak rear deck, and an interior in pale leather and navy tartan — Registered Tartan no. 14107, woven exclusively for Outlander by Lochcarron of Scotland.

The vehicle had to feel effortless at 20 mph on a beach track and entirely at home parked outside a harbour restaurant. That tension — rugged and refined — is where Outlander does its best work.

The restoration process

We do not restore Defenders to look new. We restore them to reveal what was always there — and to place inside them a standard of craftsmanship that did not exist when they left the factory.

Assessment

Every vehicle arrives with a history. We document chassis condition, previous repairs and original specification before a single panel is removed.

Stripping & chassis

The body comes off. The chassis is media-blasted, inspected, repaired and hot-dip galvanised. This is the foundation everything else depends on.

Body & paint

Panels are aligned by hand. Gaps are set by eye. Paint is applied in our own booth — not outsourced, not rushed.

Interior & trim

Walnut consoles, tweed seat panels, tartan headlinings, teak load floors — made in-house or by the same Scottish suppliers we have used for fifteen years.

Final assembly

Engine, drivetrain, electrics, weather seals. Then a road test on Scottish roads before the vehicle is signed off.

A typical commission takes between six and eighteen months. We keep clients informed throughout — photographs from the workshop, decisions on materials, progress at every stage.
